No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

No homeowner likes surprises on the final bill. So Wenatchee HVAC quotes every Wenatchee job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know the problem, what it takes to fix, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

Most HVAC failures send small signals well before the system stops. Watch for rising utility bills, airflow that feels weaker than it used to, frost on the copper lines, or rooms that never catch up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually slow refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. A quick inspection at the first sign usually costs far less than a failed compressor.

Do you know how old your system is? Once an air conditioner or furnace passes the 12-year mark, it's worth thinking about the next one before the current one decides for you. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when choices are limited and everyone is uncomfortable. Our technicians know how the Wenatchee climate wears on equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep your home comfortable year-round. Regular service heads off failures before they start, keeps coils and burners clean, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Wenatchee HVAC, every plan is built around your home's specific equipment, with scheduled visits that cover coil cleaning, refrigerant charge verification, capacitor and contactor testing, burner and heat exchanger inspection, condensate drain clearing, filter replacement, and a static pressure check across the duct system.

Most homes do best with two visits a year: a springtime tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which clog condenser coils and push up energy use quickly.

The savings show up quickly. Clean coils move heat with less effort, a correct refrigerant charge takes strain off the compressor, and a fresh filter spares the blower motor. Next to the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Right-Sized Systems

Comfort Sized to Your Home's Demand

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Wenatchee HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation that accounts for square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Choosing new equipment comes down to more than a SEER2 number. We'll compare furnace-and-AC setups with heat pumps, and ducted systems with ductless mini-splits, walking through operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Families with a growing household frequently do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Common HVAC Problems We Fix in Wenatchee

Warm air from the vents, a furnace that won't ignite, ice on the indoor coil, a slow refrigerant leak: each of these problems has its own cause and calls for a different repair. Wenatchee HVAC diagnoses the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that short-cycles, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A weak capacitor just keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can let combustion gases into the house, and a backed-up condensate drain can soak through a ceiling before anyone notices. We come prepared to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak is fixed.

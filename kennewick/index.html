Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or gives off a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a far bigger repair.

How Kennewick Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number tells you a lot. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that point and is also showing rising bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install when the first heat wave hits.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Kennewick

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips every few days, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. Those are early signs of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Left alone, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.
